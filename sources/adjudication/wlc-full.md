# The LARGER CATECHISM, Agreed upon by the Assembly of Divines at Westminster, To be a part of Uniformity in Religion between the Churches of Christ in the three Kingdomes.

## WLC 1

*Q. 1. WHat is the chief and highest end of man?*

A. Mans chief and highest end is, to glorifie God [marg.: Rom. 11. 36. 1 Cor. 10. 31], and fully to enjoy him forever [marg.: Ps. 73. 24. to the end. Ioh. 17, 21, 22, 23.].

## WLC 2

*Q. 2. How doth it appear that there is a God?*

A. The very light of nature in man, and the works of God, declare plainly that there is a God [marg.: Rom. 1. 19 20. Ps. 19. 1. 2. 3 Acts 17. 18]: but his Word and Spirit only doe sufficiently and effectually reveale him unto men for their saltion [marg.: 1 Cor. 2 9 10 2 Tim 3. 15, 16, 17 Isa. 59. 21].

## WLC 3

*Q. 3. What is the Word of God?*

A. The holy Scriptures of the Old and New Testament are the Word of God [marg.: 2 Tim. 3 16 2 Pet. 1. 19, 20, 21], the only Rule of Faith & Obedience [marg.: Eph 2. 20 Rev. 22. 18, 19.].

## WLC 4

*Q. 4. How doth it appear that the Scriptures are the Word of God?*

A. The Scriptures manifest themselves to be the Word of God, by their majesty [marg.: Hos. 8. 12 1 Cor. 2. 6, 7 13. Psal. 119. 18, 129], and purity [marg.: Psal. 12 6 Ps. 119. 140]; by the consent of all the parts [marg.: Acts 10. 43 Acts 26. 22], and the scope of the whole, which is to give all glory to God [marg.: Rom. 3. 19, 27]; by their light and power to convince and convert sinners, to comfort and build up Believers unto salvation [marg.: Acts 18. 28 Heb. 4. 12 Iam. 1. 18 Psal. 19. 7, 8, 9. Rom. 15. 4 Acts 20. 32]: But the Spirit of God, bearing witnesse by and with the Scriptures in the heart of man, is alone able fully to perswade it, that they are the very word of God [marg.: Ioh. 16 13. 14 1 Ioh. 2. 20 1•. Ioh. 20. 31].

## WLC 5

*Q. 5. What doe the Scriptures principally teach?*

A. The Scriptures principally teach, what man is to believe concerning God, and what duty God requires of man [marg.: 2 Tim. 1. 13].

## WLC 6

*Q. 6. What doe the Scriptures make known of God?*

A. The Scriptures make known what God is [marg.: Heb. 11. 6], the Persons in the Godhead [marg.: 1 Ioh. 5. 7], his Decrees [marg.: Acts 15, 14 15, 18], and the execution of his Decrees [marg.: Acts 4. 27, 28].

## WLC 7

*Q. 7. What is God?*

A. God is a Spirit [marg.: Ioh. 4. 24], in and of himself infinite in being [marg.: Exod. 3. 14 Iob 11. •, 8, 9], glory [marg.: Acts 7. 21], blessedness [marg.: 1 Tim. 6 15.], and perfection [marg.: Mat. 5. 45]; alsufficient [marg.: Gen. 17. 1], eternal [marg.: Psal. 90. 2], unchangeable [marg.: Mal. 3. 6 Iam. 1. 17], in comprehensible [marg.: 1 Kin. 8. 27], every where present [marg.: Psal. 139. 1 to 13.], almighty [marg.: Rev. 4. 8], knowing all things [marg.: Heb. 4. 13 Psal. 147. 5], most wise [marg.: Rom. 16 17], most holy [marg.: Isa. 6. 3 Rev. 15, 4], most just [marg.: Deut. 32, 4], most mercifull and gracious, long-suffering and abundant in goodnesse and truth [marg.: Exod 34 6].

## WLC 8

*Q. 8. Are there more Gods then one?*

A. There is but one on•y, the living and true God [marg.: Deut. •. 4 1 Cor. 8. 4. 6 Ier. 10. 10].

## WLC 9

*Q. 9. How many persons are there in the Godhead?*

A. There be three Persons in the Godhead, the Father, the Son, and the Holy Ghost, and these three are one, true, eternall God, the same in substance, equall in power and glory, although distinguished by their Personall properties [marg.: 1 Ioh. 5. 7 Mat 3. 16, 17 Mat. 28. 12 2 Cor. 13. 14 Ioh. 10. 30].

## WLC 10

*Q. 10. What are the Personall Properties of the three Persons in the Godhead?*

A. It is proper to the Father to be get the Son [marg.: Heb. 1. 5, 6, 8], and to the Son to be begotten of the Father [marg.: Ioh. 1. 14, 18], and to the Holy Ghost to proceed from the Father and the Son, from all eternity [marg.: Io•. 15. 16 Gal. 4. 6].

## WLC 11

*Q. 11. How doth it appeare that the Son and the Holy Ghost are God, equall with the Father?*

A. The Scriptures manifest that the Son, and the Holy Ghost are God equall with the Father, ascribing unto them such Names [marg.: Isa. 6. 3, 5, 8 with Ioh. 12. 41. and Acts 28. 25 1 Ioh. 5, 20 Acts 5. 3, 4], attributes [marg.: Ioh. 1. 1 Isa. 9 6. Ioh. 1. 24, 25 1 Cor. 2. 10, 11.], works [marg.: Col. 1. 16 Gen 1. 2], and worship, as are proper God onely [marg.: Mat. 28. 19 2 Chr. 13. 14].

## WLC 12

*Q. 12. What are the Decrees of?*

A. Gods Decrees are the wise, free, and holy acts of the counsel of his will [marg.: Eph. 1. 11 Rom. 11. 33 Rom. 9. 14, 15, 18], whereby from all eternity, he hath for his own glory, unchangeably, fore-ordained whatsoever comes to passe in time [marg.: Eph. 1. 4 11. Rom. 9 22, 23 Psal. 33. 11]; especially concerning Angels and men.

## WLC 13

*Q. 13. What hath God especially decreed concerning Angels and Men?*

A. God by an eternall and immutable Decree, out of his meer love, for the praise of his glorious grace to bee manifested in due time, hath elected some Angels to glory [marg.: 1 Tim. 5 21]; and in Christ hath chosen some men to eternall life, and the meanes thereof [marg.: Eph. 1. 4 5, 6. 2 Thes. 2. 13 14.]: and also according to his soveraign power, and the unsear•hable counsell of his own will, (whereby he extendeth or with-holdeth favour as he pleaseth) hath passed by and fore ordained the rest to dishonour and wrath, to be for their sin inflicted, to the praise of the glory of his justice [marg.: Rom. 9. 17 18, 21, 22 Mat. 11, 25 26. 2 Tim. 2. 20. Iude v 4. 1 Pet. 2. 8.].

## WLC 14

*Q. 14. How doth God execute his Decrees?*

A. God executeth his Decrees in the works of Creation and Providence; according to his infallible foreknowledge, and the free and immutable counsell of his own will [marg.: Eph. 1. 1•].

## WLC 15

*Q. 15. What is the work of Creation?*

A. The work of Creation, is that, wherein God did, in the beginning, by the word of his Power, make of nothing, the World and all things therein, for himselfe, within the space of six dayes, and all very good [marg.: Gen. 1. Ch. Heb. 11. 3 Prov. 16. 4].

## WLC 16

*Q. 16. How did God create the Angels?*

A. God created all the Angels [marg.: Col. 1. 16], spirits [marg.: Ps 104 4], immortall [marg.: Mat. 2. 30], holy [marg.: Mat. 25. 31], excelling in knowledge [marg.: 2 Sam. 14 17. Mat. 24 36], mighty in power [marg.: 2 Thes. 1. 7], to execute his Commandements, and to praise his Name [marg.: Ps. 103. 20 21.]; yet subject to change [marg.: 2 Pet. 2 4].

## WLC 17

*Q. 17. How did God create man?*

A. After God had made all other creatures, he crated man male and female [marg.: Gen 1. 27]; formed the body of the man of the dust of the ground [marg.: Gen. 2. 7]; and the woman of a rib of the man [marg.: Gen. 2. 22]; indued them with living, reasonable, and immortall souls [marg.: Gen. 2. 7 with Iob 35 11. and Eccl. 12. 7 & Mat. 10 28. & Luk. 23. 43]; made them after his own image [marg.: Gen. •. 17], in knowledge [marg.: Col. 3. 10], righteousnesse, and holinesse [marg.: Eph 4. 14], having the Law of God written in their hearts [marg.: Rom. 1. 14 25.]: and power to fulfill it [marg.: Eccl. 7. 29], with dominion over the creatures [marg.: Gen. 1. 28], yet subject to fall [marg.: Gen. 3. 6 Eccl. 7. 29].

## WLC 18

*Q. 18. What are Gods works of Providence?*

A. Gods works of Providence are, his most holy [marg.: Ps. 145. 17], wise [marg.: Psal. 104 24 Isa 28. 19], and powerfull, preserving [marg.: Heb. 1. 3], and powerfull, preserving [marg.: Heb. 1. 3] and governing all his creatures [marg.: Mat. 10. 29 30, 31 Gen 45. 7], ordering them and all their actions a to his own glory [marg.: Rom. 11. 36 Isa 63 14].

## WLC 19

*Q. 19. What is Gods Providence toward the Angels?*

A. God by his providence permitted some of the Angels, wilfully and irrecoverably to fall into sin and damnation [marg.: Iude v. 6 2 Pet. 2. 4 Heb, 2. 16 Ioh. 8. 44], limiting and ordering that, and all their sins to his own glory [marg.: Iob 1. 12 Mat. 8. 31]; and established the rest in holinesse and happiness [marg.: • Tim. 5. 21 Mark. 8. 38 Heb. 12. 22]: imploying them all [marg.: Ps. 104. 4], at his pleasure, in the administrations of his power, mercy, and justice [marg.: 2 King. 19 35 Heb, 1. 14].

## WLC 20

*Q. 20. What was the Providence of God toward man in the estate in which he was •reated?*

A. The providence of God toward man, in the estate in which he was created, was, the placing him in Paradise, appointing him to dresse it, giving him l•berty to eat of the fruit of the earth [marg.: Gen. 1. 8, 15. 16], putting the creatures under his dominion [marg.: Gen. 1. 28], and ordaining marriage for his help [marg.: Gen. 2. 18]: affording him communion with himself [marg.: Gen. 1. 26, 27, 28. 29 Gen. 3. 8], instituting the Sabbath [marg.: Gen. 2. 3], entering into a Covenant of life with him, upon condition of personall, perfect, and perpetuall obedience [marg.: Gal 3. 12 Rom. 10. 5], of which the Tree of Life was a pledge [marg.: Gen. 2. 9], and forbidding to eat the Tree of the Knowledge of good and evil, upon pain of death [marg.: Gen. 1. 17].

## WLC 21

*Q. 21. Did man continue in that estate wherein God at first created him?*

A. Our first Parents being left to the freedome of their own will, through the temptation of Satan transgressed the Commandement of God, in eating the forbidden fruit, and thereby fell from the estate of Innocency, wherein they were created [marg.: Gen. 3. 6, 7, 8, 13 Eccl. 7, 29 2 Cor. 11. 3].

## WLC 22

*Q. 22. Did all mankinde fall in that first trangression?*

A. The Covenant being made with Adam as a publ•ke person, not for himself only, but for his posterity, all mankinde descending from him by ord•nary generation [marg.: Act 17. 26], sinned in him, and fell wi•h him in that first tran•gression [marg.: Gen. 2. 16, 17. with Rom. 5. 12 to 20 and 1 Cor 15. 21 22.].

## WLC 23

*Q. 23. Into what estate did the fall bring mankinde?*

A. The fall brought mankinde into an estate of sin and misery [marg.: Rom. •. 12 Rom. 3. 23].

## WLC 24

*Q. 24. What is sin?*

A. Sin is any want of conformity unto, or transgression of any Law of God, given as a rule to the reasonable creature [marg.: 1 Ioh. 3. 4 Gal. 3. 10. 12]

## WLC 25

*Q. 25. Wherein consisteth the sinfulnesse of that estate whereinto man fell?*

A. The sinfulness of that estate wherinto man fell, consisteth in that guilt of Adams first sin [marg.: Rom. 5. 12 19.], the want of that righteousnesse wherein he was creared, and the corruption of his nature, whereby he is utterly indisposed, disabled, and made opposite unto all that is spiritually good, and wholly inclined to all evill, and that continually [marg.: Rom. 3. 10 •0 20 Eph. 2. 1, 2, 3 Rom. 5. 6 Rom. 8. 7, 8 Gen. 6. 5], which is commonly called Originall Sin, and from which doe proceed all actuall transgressions [marg.: Iam. 1. 14, 15. Mat. 15. 19].

## WLC 26

*Q. 26. How is Originall Sin conveyed from our first Parents unto their posterity?*

A. Originall Sin is conveyed from our first Parents unto their posterity by naturall generation, so as all that proceed from them in that way, are conceived and born in sin [marg.: Psal 51. 5 Iob 14. 4 Iob 15. 14 Ioh. 3. 6].

## WLC 27

*Q. 27. What misery did the fall bring upon mankinde?*

A. The Fall brought upon mankinde the losse of Communion with God a, his [marg.: Gen 3. 8, 10, 24] displeasure and curse, so as we are by nature children of wrath [marg.: Eph. 2. 2, 3], bond slaves to Satan [marg.: 2 Tim. 2 26.], and justly liable to all punishments in this world, and that which is to come [marg.: Gen. 2. 17 Iam. 3. 39 Rom. 6. 23 Mat. 25. 41, 46. Iude v. 7].

## WLC 28

*Q. 28. What are the punishments of Sinne in this world?*

A. The punishments of Sin in this world, are, either inward, as blindnesse of mind [marg.: Eph. 4. 18], a reprobate sense [marg.: Rom. 1, 28], strong delusions [marg.: 2 Thes. 2 11.], hardness of heart [marg.: Rom. 2. 5], horror of conscience [marg.: Isa. 33. 14 Gen. 4. 13 Mat. 27. 4], & vile affections [marg.: Rom. 1. 26]: or outward, as the curse of God upon the creatures for our sakes [marg.: Gen. 3. 17], and all other evils that befall us in our bodies, names, estates, relations, and imployments [marg.: Deut. 28 15. to the end.], together with death it selfe [marg.: Rom. 6, 21 23.].

## WLC 29

*Q. 29. What are the punishments of Sin in the world to come?*

A. The punishments of Sin in the world to come, are everlasting separation from the comfortable presence of God, and most grievous torments in soul and body without intermission, in hell fire, for ever [marg.: 2 Thes. 1. 9 Mar. 9. 44 46 48 Luk. 16. 24].

## WLC 30

*Q. 30. Doth God leave all mankinde to perish in the estate of sinne and misery?*

A. God doth not leave all mankinde to perish in the estate of sin and misery [marg.: 1 Thes. 5. 9] into which they fell by the breach of the first Covenant, commonly called the Covenant of Works [marg.: Gal. 3. 10 12.]; but, of his meer love and mercy, delivereth his Elect out of it, and bringeth them into an estate of salvation by the second Covenant commonly called the Covenant of Grace [marg.: Tit. 3. 4, 5 6, 7. Gal. 3. 21 Rom. 3. 20 21, 22.].

## WLC 31

*Q. 31. With whom was the Covenant of Grace made?*

A. The Covenant of Grace was made with Christ, as the second Adam, and in him, with all the Elect, as his seed [marg.: Gal. 3. 16 Rom. •. 15 to the end. Isa. 35. 10, 11].

## WLC 32

*Q. 32. How is the Grace of God manifested in the second Covenant?*

A. The grace of God is manifested in the second Covenant, in that he freely provideth and offereth to sinners a Mediator [marg.: Gen. 3. 15 Isa. 42. 6 Ioh. 6. 27], and life and salvation by him [marg.: 1 Ioh. 5. 11 12]; and, requiring Faith as the condition to interest them in him [marg.: Ioh 3. 16 Ioh. 1. 12], promiseth and giveth his holy Spirit [marg.: Prov. 1. 23] to all his elect to work in them that faith [marg.: 2 Cor. 4. 13], with all other saving graces [marg.: Gal 5. 22 23], and to enable them unto all holy obedience [marg.: Ezek. 36 2•], as the evidence of the truth of their faith [marg.: Iam. 2. 18 22] and thankfulnesse to God [marg.: 2 Cor. 5. 14 15], and as the way which he hath appointed them to salvation [marg.: Eph. 2. 10].

## WLC 33

*Q. 33. Was the Covenant of Grace alwayes administred after one and the same manner?*

A. The Covenant of Grace was not alwayes administred after the same manner, but the administrations of it under the Old Testament were different from those under the New [marg.: 2 Cor. 3, 6 7, 8, 9].

## WLC 34

*Q. 34. How was the Covenant of Grace administred under the Old Testament?*

A. The Covenant of Grace was administred under the Old Testament, by promises [marg.: Rom 15. 8], prophecies [marg.: Act 3 20 24.], Sacrifices [marg.: Heb. 10. 1], Circumcision [marg.: Rom 4. 11], the Passeover [marg.: 1 Cor. 5. 7], and other types and ordinances, which did all foresignifie Christ then to come, and were for that time sufficient to build up the Elect in faith in the promised Messiah [marg.: Heb. 8. 9, 10 Chapters Heb 11. 13.], by whom they then had full remission of sin, and eternall salvation [marg.: Gal. 3. 7. 8, 9. 14].

## WLC 35

*Q. 35. How is the Covenant of Grace admistred under the New Testament?*

A. Vnder the new Testament, when Christ the substance was exhibited, the same Covenant of Grace was and still is to be administred in the preaching of the word [marg.: Mar. 16. 15], and the administration of the Sacraments of Baptism [marg.: Mat 28. 19 20] and the Lords Supper [marg.: 1 Cor. 11 23 24, 25]; in which, Grace and salvation is h•ld forth in more fulnesse, evidence, and efficacy, to all nations [marg.: 2 Cor. 3. 6 to the end Heb. 8. 6, 10 11. Mat. 28. 19.].

## WLC 36

*Q. 36. Who is the Mediator of the Covenant of Grace?*

A. The only Mediator of the Covenant of Grace is the Lord Iesus Christ [marg.: 1 Tim. 2. 5], who being the eternall Son of God, of one substance and equall with the Father [marg.: Ioh. 1. 1, 14 Ioh. 10. 30 Phil. 2 6], in the fulnesse of time became man [marg.: Gal 4. 4], and so was and continues to be God and man in two intire distinct natures, and one person, for ever [marg.: Luk. 1. 35 Rom. 9. 5 Col. 2. 9 Heb. 7. 14, 25].

## WLC 37

*Q. 37. How did Christ, being God, become man?*

A. Christ the Son of God became Man by taking to himselfe a true body, and a reasonable soul [marg.: Ioh. 1. 14 Mat. 26. 38], being conceiyed by the power of the Holy Ghost, in the womb of the Virgin Mary, of her substance, and born of her [marg.: Luk. 1. 27 31, 35, 42 Gal 4. 4], yet without sin [marg.: Heb. 4 5 Heb. 7. 16].

## WLC 38

*Q. 38. Why was it requisite, that the Mediator should be God?*

A. It was requisite that the Mediator should be God, that he might sustain and keep the humane nature from sinking under the infinite wrath of God, and the power of death [marg.: Acts 2. 24 25 Rom. 1. 4 with Rom. 4. 25. Heb. 9. 14]; give worth and efficacy to his suffrings, obedience and intercession [marg.: Acts 20. 28 Heb. 9. 14 Heb. 7. 25, 26, 27, 28]; and so satisfie Gods justice [marg.: Rom. 3. 24 25, 26], procure his favour [marg.: Eph. 1. 6 Mat. 3. 17], purchase a peculiar people [marg.: Tit. 2. 13 14.], give his Spirit to them [marg.: Gal. 4. 6], conquer all their enemies [marg.: Luk. 1. 68, 69, 71, 74], and bring them to everlasting salvation [marg.: Heb. 5. 8. 9 Heb. 9. 11 to 16].

## WLC 39

*Q. 39. Why was it requisite that the Mediator should be Man?*

A. It was requisite that the Mediator should be Man, that he might advance our nature [marg.: Heb. 2. 16], perform obedience to the Law [marg.: Gal. 4. 4], suffer and make intercession for us in cur nature [marg.: Heb. 2. 14 Heb. 7. 24, 25▪], have a fellow-feeling of our infirmities [marg.: Heb. 4. 15]; that we might receive the adoption of sons [marg.: Gal 4. 5], and have comfort and accesse with boldness unto the throne of Grace [marg.: Heb. 4. 16].

## WLC 40

*Q. 40. Why was it requisite that the Mediator should be God and man in one person?*

A. It was requisite that the Mediator, who was to reconcile God and man, should himselfe be both God and man, and, this in one person, that the proper works of each nature might be accepted of God for us [marg.: Mat. 1. 21. 23 Mat. 3. 17 Heb. 9. 14], and relyed on by us, as the works of the whole person [marg.: 1 Pet. 2. 6].

## WLC 41

*Q. 41. Why was our Mediator called Iesus?*

A. Our Mediator was called Iesus, because he saveth his people from their sins [marg.: Mat. 1. 2].

## WLC 42

*Q. 42. Why was our Mediator called Christ?*

A. Our Mediator was called Christ, because he was anointed with the Holy Ghost above measure [marg.: Ioh. 3 34 Psal. 45. 7], and so, set apart, and fully furnished with all authority and ability [marg.: Ioh. 6. 27 Mat. 28. 18, 19. 20.] to execute the Offices of Prophet [marg.: Acts 3. 21, 22. Luk 4. 18, 21], Priest [marg.: Heb. 5. 5, 6, 7. Heb. 4. 14, 15.], and King of his Church [marg.: Psal. 2. 6 Mat. 21. 5 Isa. 9. 6, 7 Phil. 2. 8, 9 10, 11], in the estate both of his Humiliation and Exaltation.

## WLC 43

*Q. 43. How doth Christ execute the Office of a Prophet?*

A. Christ executeth the office of a Prophet, in his revealing to the Church [marg.: Ioh. 1. 18], in all ages, by his Spirit and Word [marg.: 1 Pet. 1. 10 11, 22], in divers wayes of administration [marg.: Heb. 1. 1, 2], the whole will of God [marg.: Ioh. 15, 15] in all things concerning their edification and salvation [marg.: Acts 20. 32 Eph 4. 11, 12 13. Ioh. 20. 32].

## WLC 44

*Q. 44. How doth Christ execute the O••••e of a Priest?*

A. Christ executeth the Office of a Priest, in his once offering himselfe a Sacrifice without spot to God [marg.: Heb. 9. 14, 28.], to be a reconciliation for the sins of his people [marg.: Heb. 2. 17], and in making continuall intercession for them [marg.: Heb. 7. 25].

## WLC 45

*Q. 45. How doth Christ execute the Office a King?*

A. Christ executeth the Office of a King, in calling out of the world a people to himselfe [marg.: Acts 15 14 15, 16 Isai 55 4, 5 Gen 49. •0 Psal. 110, 3], and giving them Officers [marg.: Eph. 4. 11 12 1 Cor, 12. 28 Isai 33 22], Laws h, and Censures, by which he visibly governs them [marg.: Mat. 18. 17, 18 1 Cor. 5. 4, 5]; in bestowing saving grace upon his elect [marg.: Acts 5. 31], rewarding their obedience [marg.: Rev. 22, 12 Rev, 2. 10], and correcting them for their sins [marg.: Rev. 2. 19], preserving & supporting them under all their temptations and sufferings [marg.: Isai 63, 9], restraining and overcomming all their enemies [marg.: 1 Cor. 15 25 Psal. 110 thoughout], and powerfully ordering all things for his own glory [marg.: Rom. 14. 10, 11], and their good [marg.: Rom, 8, 28]; and also in taking vengeance on the rest, who know not God, and obey not the Gospel [marg.: 1 Thes, 1 8 9 Psal. 2, 8, 9].

## WLC 46

*Q. 46. What was the estate of Christs Humiliation?*

A. The estate of Christs Humiliation was, that low condition, wherein, he, for our sakes, emptying himself of his glory, took upon him the form of a servant in his conception & birth, life, death, and aft•• his death untill his resurrection [marg.: Phil, 2, 6, 7, 8 Luk. 1. 33 2 Cor, 8, 6 Acts 2. 2, 4].

## WLC 47

*Q. 47. How did Christ humble himself in his Conception and Birth?*

A. Christ humbled himself in his conception and birth, in that, being, from all eternity, the Son of God, in the bosome of the Father, he was pleased in the fulnesse of time to become the Son of man, made of a woman of low estate, and to be born of her, with divers circumstances of more then ordinary abasement [marg.: Iohn 1. 14. 18. Gal. 4. 4 Luke 2. 7].

## WLC 48

*Q. 48. How did Christ humble himself in his life?*

A. Christ humbled himself in his life, by subjecting himself to the Law [marg.: Gal 4. 4] which he perfectly fulfilled [marg.: Mat. 5. 17 Rom. 5. 19], and by conflicting with the indignities of the world [marg.: Psal. 22. 6 Heb. 12. 2, 3], temptations of Sathan [marg.: Mat. 4. 1. 10 12. Luke 4. 13], and infirmities in his flesh, whether common to the nature of man or particularly accompanying that his low condition [marg.: Heb. 2. 17, 18 Heb. 4. 15 Isai 5, 13, •4.].

## WLC 49

*Q. 49. How did Christ humble himself in his death?*

A. Christ humbled himselfe in his death, in that having been betrayed by Iudas [marg.: Mat. 27. 4.], forsaken by his Disciples [marg.: Mat. 20. 56], scorned and rejected by the world [marg.: Isa 53 2 3], condemned by Pilate, and tormented by his persecutors [marg.: Mat. 27. 26 to 50 Iohn 19. 34▪]; having also conflicted with the terrors of death, and the power of darknesse, felt and born the weight of Gods wrath [marg.: Luk, 22 44 Mat 27. 46]; he laid down his life an offering for sin [marg.: Ira. 53. 10], enduring the painfull, shameful and cursed death of the crosse [marg.: Phil. 2. 8 Heb. 12. 2 Gal. 3. 13.].

## WLC 50

*Q. 50. Wherein consisted Christs humiliation after his death?*

A. Christs humiliation after his death, consisted in his being buried [marg.: 1 Cor. 15 3. 4 Psal 16. 10 with Acts 2 24, 25, 26, 27 31 Rom. 6. 9 Mat. 12. 40.], & continuing in the state of the dead, & under the power of death till the third day i, which hath been other Wise expressed in these words, He descended into Hell.

## WLC 51

*Q. 51. What was the estate of Christs exaltation?*

A. The estate of Christs Exaltation comprehendeth his Resurrection [marg.: 1 Cor. 15 4], Asscention [marg.: Mar. 16. 19.], sitting at the right hand of the Father [marg.: Eph 1. 20.], and his coming again to judge the world [marg.: Acts 1. 11. Acts 17. 31.].

## WLC 52

*Q. 52. How was Christ exalted in his Resurrection?*

A. Christ was exalted in his Resurrection, in that, not having seen corruption in death, of which it was not possible for him to be held [marg.: Acts 2. 24. 27.]; and having the very same body in which he suffered, with the essentiall properties thereof [marg.: Luk. 24. 39], but without mortality and other common infirmities belonging to this life, really united to his soule [marg.: Rom. 10. 9. A. Although no man, since the fall, can attain to righteousnesse and life by the Morall Law [marg.: Rom. 8. 3 Gal. 2. 16], yet there is great use thereof, as well common to all men, as peculiar either to the unregenerate, or the regenerate [marg.: 1 Tim. 1. 8].

## WLC 95

*Q. 95. Of what use is the Morall Law to all men?*

A. ## WLC 96

A. The Morall Law is of use to unregenerate men, to awaken their consciences to fly from wrath to come [marg.: 1 Tim. 1. 9 10], and to drive them to Christ [marg.: Gal. 1. 24]; or, upon their continuance in the estate and way of sin, to leave them inexcusable [marg.: Rom. 1. 20 with Rom. 2. 15 Gal. 3. 10.], and under the curse thereof e.

## WLC 97

## WLC 98

## WLC 99

*Q. 99. What rules are to be observed for the right understanding of the ten Commandements?*

A. For the right understanding of the Commandements, these rules are to be observed.

That the Law is perfect, and bindeth every one to ful conformity in the whole man unto the righteousnes therof, & unto intire obedience, for ever; so as, to require the utmost perfectiō of every duty, & to forbid the least degree of every sin [marg.: Psal. 19. 7 Iam. •. 10 Mat. 5. 21 to the end.].

That it is spirituall; and so, reacheth the Vnderstanding, Will, Affections, and all other powers of the soul, as well as words, works, and gestures [marg.: Rom. 7. 14 Deut 6. 5 with Mat. 22, 37▪ 38, 39 Mat. 5. 21, 22 27 28, 36, to the end.].

That one and the same thing, in divers respects, is required or forbidden in severall Commandements [marg.: Col. 3 5 Amos 8. 5 Prov. 1. 19 1 Tim. 6. 10].

That, as, where a duty is commanded, the contrary sin is forbidden [marg.: Isa. 58. 13 Deu. 6. 13 with Mat. 4, 9, 10 Mat 15. 4 5, 6]; and, where a sin is forbidden, the contrary duty is commanded [marg.: Mat. 5. 21, 22, 23, 24, 25 Eph. 4. 28]; so, where a promise is annexed, the contrary threatning is included [marg.: Exo. 10. 12 with Prov. 30, 17]; and, where a threatning is annexed, the contrary promise is included [marg.: Ier. 18. 7, 8 Exod 20. 7, with Ps 15 1, 4, 5, & Psal. 24. 4, 5].

That, what God forbids, is at no time to be done [marg.: Iob 13. •, • Rom. 3. 8 Iob 36, 21 Heb 11. 25]; what he commands, is alwayes our duty [marg.: Deut. 4. 8. 9], yet every particular duty is not to be done at all times [marg.: Mat. 12. 7].

That, under one sin or duty, all of the same kind are forbidden or commanded, together with all the causes, meanes, occasions, and appearances thereof, and provocations thereunto [marg.: Mat. 5. 21 22, 27, 28 Mat. 15 4, 5. 6 Heb. 10 24, 25 1 Thes. 5. 22 Iude v. 13 Gal. 5. 16 Col. 3. 21].

That what is fo•bidden or commanded to our selves, we are bound, according to our places, to endeavour that it may be avoyded or performed by others, according to the duty of their places [marg.: Exod. 20 10 Lev. 19. 17 Gen. 18. 19 Iosh 24. 15 Deut. 6. 6, 7].

The duty of Christians after they have received the Sacrament of the Lords Supper, is, seriously to consider how they have behaved themselves therein, and with what successe [marg.: Psal 28. 7 Psal. 85. 8 2 Cor. 11. 17 30. 31]; if they find quickning & comfort, to blesse God for it [marg.: 2 Chr. 30 21 22, 23, 25, 26 Acts 2. 42 46. 47], beg the continuance of it [marg.: Psal. 36. 10 Cant. 3. 4 1 Chr. 29. 18], watch against relapses [marg.: 1 Cor. 10. 3 4, 5, 12], fulfill their vows [marg.: Psal. 50. 14] & incourage themselves to a frequent attendance on that ordinance [marg.: 1 Cor. 11. 25 26 Act. 2. 42. 46]: but if they find no present benefit, more exactly to review their preparation to, & carriage at the Sacrament [marg.: Cant. 5. 1. 2, 3, 4, 5, 6.], in both which if they can approve themselves to God & their own cōsciences, they are to wait for the fruit of it in due time [marg.: Psal. 123. Psal. 42. 5, 8 Ps. 43. 3, 4, 5]: but if they see they have failed in either, they are to be humbled [marg.: 2 Chr. 30 18. 19 Isa. 1. 16. 18], and to attend upon it afterward with more care and diligence [marg.: 2 Cor. 7. 11 1 Chr. 15, 12 13. 14].

## WLC 176

*Q. 176. Wherein do the Sacraments of Baptisme and the Lords Supper agree?*

A. The Sacraments of Baptism and the Lords Supper agree, in that the authour of both is God [marg.: Mat. 28. 19. 1 Cor 11. 23▪], the spirituall part of both is Christ and his benefits [marg.: Rom. 6, 3. 4 1 Cor. 10. 16], both are seals of the same Covenant [marg.: Rom. 4. 11 with Col. 2 12 Mat. 26 27. 28.], are to be dispensed by Ministers of the Gospel and by none other [marg.: Iohn 1. 33 Mat 28. 19 1 Cor. 11 23 1 Cor. 4. 1 Heb. •. 4.], & to be continued in the Church of Christ, untill his second comming [marg.: Mat. 28. 19 20. 1 Cor. 11. 26].

## WLC 177

*Q. 177. Wherein doe the Sacraments of Baptisme and the Lords Supper differ?*

A. The Sacraments of Baptism and the Lords Supper differ, in that Baptism is to be administred but once, with water to be a sign and seal of our regeneration and engrafting into Christ [marg.: Mat. 3, 11 Tit, 3, 5 Cal. 3, 27], and that even to infants [marg.: Gen 17, 7, 9 Acts 2. 38. 39 1. Cor 7. 14]; whereas the Lords Supper is to be administred often, in the Elements of bread and wine, to represent and exhibit Christ as spirituall nourishment to the soul [marg.: 1 Cor, 11 23, 24, 25, 26], and to confirm our continuance and growth in him [marg.: 1 Cor. 10, 16], and that only to such as are of years and ability to examine themselves [marg.: 1 Cor, 11 28, 29.].

## WLC 178

*Q. 178. What is Prayer?*

A. Prayer is an offering up of our defires unto God [marg.: Psal. 62, 8], in the Name of Christ [marg.: Ich. 16, 23], by the help of his Spirit [marg.: Rom. 8. 26], with confession of our sins [marg.: Psa, 32, 5, 6 Dan. 9, 4], and thankfull acknowledgement of his mercies [marg.: Phil. 4. 6].

## WLC 179

*Q. 179. Are we to pray unto God only?*

A. God only being able to search the hearts [marg.: 1 Kin. 8. 39 Acts 1. 24 Rom. 8. 27], hear the requests [marg.: Psal. 65, 2], pardon the sins [marg.: Mich, 7. 18], and fulfill the desires of all [marg.: Psal. 145 18, 19], and only to be believed in [marg.: Rom. 10. 14], and worshipped with religious worship [marg.: Mat. 4. 10], prayer, which is a speciall part thereof [marg.: 1 Cor. 1. 2], is to be made by all to him alone k, and to none other [marg.: Ps. 50. 15 •om. 10. 14].

## WLC 180

*Q. 180. What is it to pray in the Name of Christ?*

A. To pray in the Name of Christ, is, in obedience to his command, and in confidence on his promises, to ask mercy for his sake [marg.: Ioh. 14 13, 14 •ohn 16. 24 Dan. 9. 17], not by bare mentioning of his Name [marg.: Mat. 7. 21], but by drawing our encouragement to pray, and our boldnesse, strength, and hope of acceptance in prayer, from Christ and his mediation [marg.: Heb. 4. 14 15. 16 1 Ioh 5. 13, 14, 15].

## WLC 181

*Q. 181. Why are wee to pray in the name of Christ?*

A. The sinfulnesse of man, and his distance from God by reason thereof, being so great as that we can have no accesse into his presence without a Mediator [marg.: Ioh. 14▪ 6 I sai 59. 2 Ephes. 3 12]; and there being none in heaven or earth appointed to, or fit for that glorious work, but Christ alone [marg.: Ioh. 6. 27 Heb. 7. 25. 26 27 1 Tim. 2. 5]; we are to pray in no other name but this only [marg.: Col 3. 17 Heb. 13. 17].

## WLC 182

*Q. 182. How doth the Spirit help us to pray?*

A. We not knowing what to pray for as we ought, the Spirit helpeth our infirmities, by enabling us to understand both for whom, and what, and how prayer is to be made, and by working and quickning in our hearts, (although not in all persons, nor at all times, in the same measure) those apprehensions, affections, and graces, which are requisite for the right performance of that duty [marg.: Rom. 8. 26 27 Psal. 10. 17 Zech 12. 10].

## WLC 183

*Q. 183. For whom are we to pray?*

A. We are to pray for the whole Church of Christ, upon earth [marg.: Eph. 6 18 Psal. 2• ▪ 9], for Migistrates [marg.: 1 Tim. •. 1, 2] and Ministers [marg.: Co•. 4. 3], for our selvs [marg.: Gen. 32. 11], our brethren [marg.: Iam. 5. 16], yea our enemies [marg.: Mat. 5. 44], and for all sorts of men living [marg.: 1 Tim. 2. 1, 2], or that shall live hereafter [marg.: Ioh, 17, 20 2 Sam. 7. 29]; but not for the dead [marg.: 2 Sam. 12 21, 22, 23], nor for those that are known to have sinned the sin unto death [marg.: 1 Ioh. 5. 16].

## WLC 184

*Q. 184. For what things are we to pray?*

A. We are to pray for all things tending to the glory of God [marg.: Mat. 6. 9], the welfare of the Church [marg.: Psal. 51. 18 Psal. 122. 6], our own [marg.: Mat. 7. 11], or others good [marg.: Psal. 125. 4], but not for any thing that is unlawfull [marg.: 1 Ioh. 5. 14].

## WLC 185

*Q. 185. How are we to pray?*

A. We are to pray, with an awfull apprehension of the Majesty of God [marg.: Eccl. 5. 1.], and deep sense of our own unworthinesse [marg.: Gen. 17. 27 Gen. 32. 19], necessities [marg.: Luk. 15. 17 18, 19]: and sins [marg.: Luk. 18 13, 14]; with penitent [marg.: Psal. 51. 17], thankful [marg.: Phil. 4. 6], and enlarged hearts [marg.: 1 Sam. 1. 15 1 Sam. 211], with understanding [marg.: 1 Cor. 14 15], faith, [marg.: Mar. 11. 24 Iames 1. 6], sincerity [marg.: Psal. 145. 18 Psal. 17. 1], fervency [marg.: Iam. 5. 16], love [marg.: 1 Tim. 2. 8], and perseverance [marg.: Eph. 6. 18], waiting upon him [marg.: Mic. 7, 7] with humble submission to his will [marg.: Mat. 26. 36]

## WLC 186

*Q. 186. What rule bath God given for our direction in the duty of prayer?*

A. The whole word of Gods is of use to direct us in the duty of praying [marg.: 1 Ioh. 5. 14]; but the speciall rule of direction is that form of prayer, which our Saviour Christ taught his Disciples, commonly called the Lords Prayer [marg.: Mat 2. 9 10, 11, 12, 13 Luk. 11, 2, 3, 4].

## WLC 187

*Q. 187. How is the Lords Prayer to be used?*

A. The Lords Prayer is not only for direction, as a pattern according to which we are to make other prayers; but may also be used as a prayer, so that it be done with understanding, faith, reverence, and other graces necessary to the right performance of the duty of prayer [marg.: Mat. 6. 9 with Luk 11. 2].

## WLC 188

*Q. 188. Of how many parts doth the Lords Prayer consist?*

A. The Lords Prayer consists of three parts, a Preface, Petitions, and a Conclusion.
